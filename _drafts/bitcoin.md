No single image has broken people's brains more than the below chart. There's two interlocking infohazards here: A) there's an asset whose value has gone up by 10,000x in ~15 years (really even more than that, depending on where you start counting); B) you were capable of getting those returns, but failed to do so. The average person sees this and it's suddenly hard for them to imagine slaving away at a job and making 40 years of retirement contributions to an index fund.

<img src="/assets/images/btcChart.png" width="500">

At this point, the shift towards the gamble-fication of all of American life is well-documented. Every young man is buying some combination of deep OTM options, same game parlays, and prediction market positions. I posit that you can trace all of this back to bitcoin. Given a percieved lack of stability and potential for advancement in the traditional labor market, the  philosophy of hedging risk via making these high-risk bets sort of makes sense. It becomes problematic if gambling is displacing actual value creation in the economy. 

And I think that's exactly what's starting to happen. Venture capitalists have traditionally made investments with a 'long tail' outlook - only one or two companies from their portfolio need to succeed to return their fund. Depending on your personal opinion about VCs, you can call that a sophisticated business model or a game of darts. That being said, the broader public markets are starting to look like this as well! 

The American dream isn't a steady job, nuclear family, two car garage, and picket fence anymore. It's the making it rich quick off of a deeply asymmetric high leverage bet.
