At the start of Dune, Paul (our 'hero') is forced to put his hand in a box. The box begins to administer enormous amounts of pain, but if Paul pulls out his hand, he will be stabbed with a poison needle (the titular gom jabbar) by the woman administering the test, who is a sort of interstellar nun/high priestess/royal eugenicist. Because he's our hero, Paul obviously passes. The test is suppoed to check if Paul is human - as 'animals' would be willing to chew through their own limbs to escape a trap.

It's sort of an underdefined premise. The novel never quite fully explains if they are testing Paul for his possession of a rare skill (as he's understood to be close to the conclusion of a multi-century breeding program) or of a basic quality. I.e., if an average citizen of the story's universe were to face the gom jabbar, would they fail? How rare is humanity here? Certainly Paul is unique in his pain tolerance, but is being human about not pulling your hand out, or not flinching into the needle, or about a threshold of pain tolerance?

I'm being pedantic, but I want to consider the countours of the test before I begin my little analogy. We live in a weird time, distinguished in part by a broad access to the internet and subsequent flattening of culture. By and large, the social and cultural elites consume the same culture & are addicted to the same apps that the proletariat are. I personally worry about my own ability to A) think critically and engage with difficult thoughts, given the easy access to extremely engaging content; B) think orthogonally to others, given that we are largely being fed the same mono-cultural inputs. 

To me, there's some aspect of humanity lost as we descend mindlessly into short-form video content (yeah real original thought, I know). So much like in Dune, we might want to filter for those who either have not lost that aspect of 'human nature' or have some innate quality that makes them more human in this aspect. One sort of reader might say that this is really just a test of intelligence -- that if you were just smart enough and had the requisite force of will, you could look away from the abyss. I don't think that's quite true: my prototypical counter-example is the mythical wood-wittling country bumpkin, who is off-line and off-the-grid, living with some sort of inner peace/harmony, but not necessarily intelligence. Then again, maybe this guy I made up would also get one-shotted by Instagram Reels. 

So the analogy: The box is not looking at your phone/the internet, and if you can withstand the boredom, you are validated as human. The gom jabbar is still the poison needle, but the test is about attention and boredom and will. I don't know if I would pass.
